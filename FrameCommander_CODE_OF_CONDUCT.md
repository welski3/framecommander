# FrameCommander Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

FrameCommander is an open-source, beginner-friendly web interface for AI video generation using MiniMax H3 / FastH3 Omni, ComfyUI workflows, and RunningHub. Our goal is to make capable video-generation workflows easier to use, understand, share, and improve—without requiring everyone to work directly with complex node graphs.

We welcome people of all experience levels, including first-time ComfyUI users, video creators, designers, developers, workflow authors, and maintainers. Everyone participating in the FrameCommander community deserves a respectful, constructive, and harassment-free environment.

## Expected behavior

When participating in FrameCommander discussions, issues, pull requests, tutorials, or other project spaces, please:

- **Be welcoming and patient.** Explain concepts clearly and respectfully, especially to newcomers unfamiliar with ComfyUI, RunningHub, API keys, or video-generation workflows.
- **Give actionable feedback.** Describe bugs, unexpected generation behavior, and compatibility issues with useful details; criticize ideas or code rather than people.
- **Respect creators and contributors.** Credit the authors of code, workflows, nodes, models, images, videos, and documentation where appropriate, and follow applicable licenses.
- **Protect privacy and credentials.** Never ask others to post their RunningHub, Gemini, or other API keys, access tokens, personal information, or private media in public issues or pull requests. Redact secrets from logs and screenshots.
- **Share reproducible technical reports.** When possible, include relevant workflow versions, settings, error messages, and steps to reproduce—without revealing secrets or sharing media you do not have permission to disclose.
- **Respect third-party platforms and tools.** Follow the rules and licensing terms of RunningHub, ComfyUI, MiniMax, model providers, custom-node authors, and other services used with FrameCommander.
- **Use AI video tools responsibly.** Do not use project spaces to coordinate harassment, impersonation, non-consensual intimate imagery, or other harmful or unlawful uses of generated media.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not permitted in FrameCommander community spaces:

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, bullying, or discriminatory remarks.
- Sexualized comments, unwanted sexual attention, or sharing sexual or violent content to target or upset others.
- Personal attacks, trolling, deliberate disruption, or repeated bad-faith arguments.
- Publishing another person's private information, credentials, private input media, or generated outputs without permission.
- Intentionally misleading contributors about workflow compatibility, performance, costs, or licensing.
- Spam, malicious links, or attempts to trick others into revealing access credentials.
- Retaliation against someone for raising a concern or reporting a violation.

Differences of opinion about AI models, generation quality, hosting costs, workflow design, or implementation choices are welcome when discussed respectfully.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to participation in the FrameCommander GitHub repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, documentation contributions, and other project-managed community spaces. It also applies when someone represents the project in a public community setting.

It governs community participation; it does not replace FrameCommander's MIT License, third-party service terms, or applicable laws.

## Reporting concerns

For ordinary bug reports, feature requests, and technical questions, please use the [FrameCommander GitHub Issues](https://github.com/welski3/framecommander/issues) page.

**Do not post sensitive Code of Conduct reports, private information, API keys, or personal evidence in a public issue.** For sensitive concerns, contact the project maintainer privately through an appropriate contact method listed on the [maintainer's GitHub profile](https://github.com/welski3). If no private contact method is available, request one without publishing the details of the incident.

Reports will be considered as confidentially as reasonably possible. The maintainer will review the available information, seek clarification when needed, and decide on a proportionate response. A specific response time is not guaranteed.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may take appropriate action to protect the community, including:

1. **Guidance or correction** for minor or unintentional violations.
2. **A warning** and a request to stop problematic conduct.
3. **Temporary restrictions** on participation for serious or repeated violations.
4. **Permanent restrictions** for severe misconduct, threats, harassment, or repeated violations.

The maintainer may edit or remove comments, close or lock discussions, decline contributions, or restrict participation in project-managed spaces when necessary. Decisions will consider the context and seriousness of the behavior. Reports should be handled fairly, without retaliation.
